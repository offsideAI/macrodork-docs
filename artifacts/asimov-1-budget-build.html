1. What Asimov-1 is

Asimov-1 is a 1.2 m, 35 kg biped with 25 powered joints. Hardware is released under CERN-OHL-S-2.0 and software under GPL-2.0. The repository carries the full mechanical CAD in seven sub-assemblies, KiCad sources for four boards, a MuJoCo MJCF and a URDF, and a fabrication manifest generator.

SubsystemSpecification
Joints6 per leg (hip pitch, roll, yaw, knee, ankle pitch, roll), 5 per arm (shoulder pitch, roll, yaw, elbow, wrist yaw), waist yaw, neck yaw and pitch. The locomotion policy runs on 23 joints with the neck locked.
AnkleParallel RSU mechanism. Two actuators A and B drive a bar; firmware maps theta_A, theta_B to pitch and roll. Fixed flat feet (v0's passive toe was dropped).
ActuatorsFive Encos quasi-direct-drive models, CAN at 1 Mbps, each with two equivalent XT30 (2+2) ports carrying BATT+, BATT−, CAN_H, CAN_L. Six daisy-chain branches enter at both hip pitches, waist yaw, both shoulder pitches and neck yaw.
Motion controlRPU: Radxa CM5 on a custom Motion Control Board with an LSM6DSV IMU and six SPI-CAN ports, plus a stacked Power Distribution Board with an XT90 (2+2) input.
MediaRaspberry Pi 5 in the head on a custom head board (buck converters, audio codec) and a small HAT. 2 MP monocular camera, stereo microphone array, one 10 W 4 Ω speaker. Direct Ethernet to the RPU.
InterfaceLiveKit / WebRTC. Protobuf commands (velocity, 25-joint trajectory with per-joint kp and kd, mode), 10 Hz telemetry, H.264 720p30 video, Opus audio. Trajectory sessions stream at about 50 Hz and fall back to DAMP on packet loss.
Power13S4P INR18650 pack, 46.8 V nominal, 10.5 Ah, 491 Wh, 30 A discharge limit, about 2 kg.
Structure7075 aluminium and MJF PA12 nylon; 43 aluminium, 18 stainless 316L and 37 nylon fabricated parts plus 67 off-the-shelf items in the manifest.
Rated loads5 kg squat; 5 kg per arm curl and front raise; 6 kg lateral raise; peaks 15 to 18 kg per arm.

That is thirty times the budget before the battery, two compute boards, four custom PCBs, 43 machined 7075 parts and 37 MJF nylon parts. The EC-A4315 price is interpolated between its neighbours; the other four are listed prices at a US reseller. No amount of substitution inside the 1.2 m frame changes this, because a 35 kg biped needs 120 N·m hips and nothing in that class costs less than a few hundred dollars.

The way to $800 is scale, not substitution. Joint torque scales with mass times lever arm. Shrinking the same geometry by a factor s cuts mass by about s³ and torque demand by about s⁴. At s = 0.40, the 120 N·m hip becomes a 3 N·m hip, which is exactly the range of $15 to $45 serial bus servos.

3. The derivative: Asimov-1 at 40 percent

Scale factor 0.40 is chosen because it is the largest size at which a 30 kg·cm class bus servo still covers most joints, and because K-Scale's Z-Bot (40 cm, about 2 kg, sixteen STS3215-class servos, roughly $500 BOM) proves a 3D-printed biped walks at this size on these servos.

STS3215 stall torque 30 kg·cm (2.9 N·m) at 12 V; STS3250 stall 50 kg·cm (4.9 N·m). Bus servos sustain roughly a third of stall continuously, so joints that carry the body in single stance get the larger servo. The scaled peak column uses Asimov-1's own published peaks, which already include its safety margins.

What is kept from Asimov-1

  • Topology. Same 25-joint layout and joint axes, so the published MJCF and URDF can be rescaled rather than remodelled, and the locomotion training guides apply unchanged. Tier A locks the neck exactly as Asimov's policy does.
  • Parallel RSU ankle. Two servos on a bar with the same A/B to pitch/roll mapping. At this size the linkages are M3 rod ends on threaded rod.
  • Six-branch daisy chain. Feetech's TTL bus carries power and data on one 3-wire cable like the XT30 (2+2) CAN chain, so the six branch entries (both hips, waist, both shoulders, neck) are reproduced with two driver boards and a small distribution board.
  • Control split. Asimov keeps the motion loop on the RPU and media on a Pi 5. The budget build runs both on one Pi 5 but keeps the same boundary in software: a real-time servo loop process and a separate LiveKit media process, matching the published protobuf command and telemetry schema.

What is given up

  • Payload. Rated loads scale to about 0.3 kg per arm. This is a locomotion and control research platform, not a lifter.
  • Torque control. Feetech STS servos offer position, velocity and current feedback but not the current-mode torque control of the Encos actuators. Policies trained with PD position targets deploy fine; torque-space policies do not.
  • Structure. FDM PETG replaces 7075 aluminium and MJF PA12. Print structural parts at 5 or more walls and 40 percent infill.
  • Bus bandwidth. One 1 Mbps TTL chain of 12 servos sustains a 50 Hz sync read and write with margin; 25 on one chain does not, hence the split into two chains.

The link shells are Asimov-1's own published simulation meshes, scaled by 0.40 and placed with the joint positions and axes from the MJCF. 6. Upgrades in order of value

  1. Hip roll to STS3250 (+$51). The 2.3 N·m scaled peak is the closest call in the table.
  2. Neck joints (+$33). Restores the full 25-joint topology so the head can track.
  3. LSM6DSV16X IMU (+$19). On-chip sensor fusion frees the Pi from the orientation filter and matches the Asimov MCB sensor.
  4. Pi Camera Module 3 (+$17). Autofocus and a sensor Raspberry Pi OS supports out of the box.
  5. Second battery (+$30). Training-data collection sessions outlast a single 3S pack.

8. Build notes

  • CAD. Rescale the Asimov-1 STEP tree by 0.40, then replace each Encos envelope with the 45.2 × 24.7 × 35 mm case shared by the STS3215 and STS3250 and re-pocket the mounts. Keep the joint axes where the MJCF puts them.
  • Simulation. Scale link lengths by 0.40, masses by the printed-part estimates, and set actuator limits from the servo stall curves. The Asimov locomotion-training guides on docs.menlo.ai apply directly; retrain, do not reuse the shipped policy.
  • Servo IDs. Assign the same 1 to 25 map Asimov uses (left leg 1 to 6, right leg 7 to 12, arms 13 to 22, waist 23, neck 24 and 25) so the firmware joint order and the trajectory message layout carry over.
  • Voltage. Run everything from the 3S pack. Do not mix 7.4 V and 12 V servo variants on one bus; the STS3250 is 12 V only.
  • Falls. Print the head and hand shells in TPU or add foam. At 2.3 kg a fall is survivable; at 35 kg Asimov relies on serviceable "off-the-shelf failure points", which the small build gets for free from cheap servos and reprintable parts.
